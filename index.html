About

I'm a PhD Candidate in Learning Sciences and Technologies at the University of Pennsylvania's Graduate School of Education, where I work in the AIED Lab and am a Penn AI Fellow. My research brings together learning analytics and AI in education. I study massive open online courses (MOOCs) and AI-based instructional systems, using statistical and machine learning methods to understand how people learn in K-12 and workforce settings.

Research Projects & Publications

My research is organized around four projects:

Student Engagement in MOOCs

How course design and social interaction shape learner engagement in MOOCs and video-based learning, from discussion forums to the placement of interesting but irrelevant "seductive details" in lectures.

  • Seductive Details in Video Lectures: Examining the Effects of Placement on Learner Engagement Mehta, S., Wong, A., Mills, C., Baker, R. S., & Isotani, S. (2026) Proceedings of the 34th International Conference on Computers in Education (ICCE) PDF
  • Seductive Details in MOOCs: Distraction or Engagement? Mehta, S., Baker, R. S., Barany, A., Vanacore, K., Andres, J., Butt, S., & Chi, J. (2026) Annual Meeting of the American Educational Research Association (AERA), Los Angeles, CA PDF
  • Do MOOC Conversations Matter? Investigating the Role of Social Presence and Course-Relevant Discussion in Career Advancement Mehta, S., Srivastava, N., Liu, X., Vanacore, K., & Baker, R. S. (2025) Proceedings of the Twelfth ACM Conference on Learning @ Scale (L@S '25), 236–240 PDF

AI as a Teaching Partner

Whether large language models can teach using established pedagogical strategies such as examples, analogies, and Socratic questioning, and what generative AI means for K-12 classrooms.

  • Can AI be a Teaching Partner? Evaluating ChatGPT, Gemini, and DeepSeek across Three Teaching Strategies de Souza, T. P. C., Mehta, S., Uema, M. A., de Paula, L. B., & Isotani, S. (under review) Preprint (PDF)
  • Generative AI Disruption in K-12 Education: Balancing Skepticism and Opportunity Mehta, S. (2026) Annual Meeting of the American Educational Research Association (AERA), Los Angeles, CA PDF

Small and Local LLMs

Whether small, open-weight language models that run locally can tutor effectively, offering a lower-cost, privacy-preserving option for schools with limited resources.

  • Can Small Language Models Teach Effectively? Evaluating Explanations and Analogies Strategies Mei, S., Meng, F., Chen, S., Mehta, S., de Souza, T. P. C., & Isotani, S. (2026) Workshop on Small Language Models for Education (SLM4ED) @ Festival of Learning 2026 PDF

Role-Play for Ethics Education

Designing role-play case studies that help engineering and computing students reason about the ethical, social, and environmental sides of everyday technology. Conducted at the TRAILS Lab, George Mason University.

  • Teaching Multidimensional Ethical Decision-Making Through a Role-Play Case Study Mehta, S., Hingle, A., & Johri, A. (2023) 2023 IEEE Frontiers in Education Conference (FIE), 1–6 PDF
  • Teaching Sustainability Through a Role-Play Case Study of E-Scooter Use on College Campuses Mehta, S., Hingle, A., & Johri, A. (2023) 51st Annual Conference of the European Society for Engineering Education (SEFI 2023) DOI
